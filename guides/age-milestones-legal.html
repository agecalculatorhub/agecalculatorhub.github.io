Legal Age Milestones Around the World: When You Can Vote, Drive and Drink

AgeCalculator guides

Age is not just a personal number — it is a legal one. The law uses age to decide when you can vote, drive, drink, marry, sign a contract and be tried as an adult. Those ages differ widely around the world, and they do not always make sense: in the United States you can vote and join the army at 18 but cannot drink until 21, and in some countries you can marry at 16 but cannot vote until 18. This guide explains the main legal milestones and where they fall.

Voting and the age of majority

The age of majority — when a person becomes a legal adult — is 18 in most of the world, including the United States, the United Kingdom, most of Europe, India, China and Australia. A few countries set it at 19, 20 or 21, including South Korea at 19 and Singapore at 21. Voting age is usually the same as the age of majority, but there are exceptions: Austria, Brazil, Cuba and several other countries allow voting at 16, and Scotland allows 16-year-olds to vote in Scottish elections.

The age of majority matters because it brings a bundle of rights and responsibilities at once: the right to sign contracts, to marry without parental permission, to make medical decisions, and to be tried as an adult in court. It also brings obligations: jury service, taxes, and liability for debts. The trend in recent decades has been to lower the voting age while keeping the age of majority at 18, on the argument that young people affected by political decisions should have a say in them.

Driving ages

The minimum driving age ranges from 14 to 18 around the world. In the United States it varies by state, from 14 in South Dakota to 16 in most states, with a learner's permit often available a year earlier. In the United Kingdom and most of Europe it is 17 or 18. In some countries, including parts of Australia and Canada, there is a graduated system: a learner's permit, then a provisional licence with restrictions, then a full licence after one or two years of safe driving.

The differences reflect different judgements about risk. Younger drivers have more accidents, and the countries with the lowest driving ages tend to have the strictest graduated systems. Some countries also set a higher age for larger vehicles: you can drive a car at 18 but need to be 21 to drive a bus or a lorry. And in a few places, including Saudi Arabia, women were only allowed to drive from 2018, a reminder that driving age is as much about culture as about safety.

Drinking, marriage and criminal responsibility

The legal drinking age is 21 in the United States, 18 in most of Europe and the United Kingdom, 19 in Canada, and 20 in Japan and South Korea. Some countries have no minimum drinking age at all, though they restrict purchase. The age of consent for marriage ranges from 16 to 18 in most countries, often with parental permission required below 18, and in some countries it is as low as 14 or 15. The age of criminal responsibility ranges from 7 in some places to 18 in others, though most countries set it between 10 and 14.

These differences can catch travellers out. A 19-year-old who can drink legally in London cannot drink legally in New York. A 17-year-old who can drive in Britain cannot drive in Germany. The practical advice is simple: when you travel, check the local age limits for the things you plan to do, and carry identification that proves your age. A passport is the most widely accepted proof, and a driving licence works in most places too.

Frequently asked questions

What is the age of majority in most countries?
18 — the age you can vote, sign contracts and marry without permission.

What is the legal drinking age in the US?
21, though it is 18 in most of Europe.

Can 16-year-olds vote anywhere?
Yes — in Austria, Brazil, Cuba, Scotland and several other places.
