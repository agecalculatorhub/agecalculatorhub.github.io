Milestone Birthdays Worth Celebrating: 18, 21, 30, 40, 50 and Beyond

AgeCalculator guides

Some birthdays matter more than others. Turning 18 brings the right to vote, turning 21 brings the right to drink in the United States, turning 30 feels like the end of youth, and turning 100 brings a letter from the monarch in some countries. These milestone birthdays are markers of a life, and they deserve to be celebrated. This guide explains what each milestone means and how to mark it.

The legal milestones: 16, 18 and 21

Sixteen is the age of consent in many countries, the age you can leave school in some, and the age you can ride a moped or drive in others. Eighteen is the big one in most of the world: the age of majority, when you can vote, sign contracts, marry without permission, and be tried as an adult. It is the birthday that turns a child into a citizen in the eyes of the law, and it is celebrated accordingly in most cultures.

Twenty-one is the drinking age in the United States and a traditional coming-of-age birthday in Britain, where it was once the age of majority and was celebrated with a key — symbolising the key to the door of adult life. In many countries, 21 is now less significant legally, but it remains a popular party birthday, especially in the United States, where it marks the end of the last major age restriction.

The decade birthdays: 30, 40 and 50

Thirty is the birthday that gets the most jokes and the most anxiety. It marks the end of your twenties, the decade of freedom and experiment, and the beginning of the decade when most people settle down, buy homes and start families. In truth, thirty is young — most people report that their thirties are happier and more confident than their twenties — but the birthday feels significant because it is the first one that sounds like a grown-up number.

Forty is traditionally the birthday of midlife, though modern forty-year-olds are healthier and more active than any previous generation. Fifty is the birthday that brings senior discounts, retirement planning and, in many cultures, a large party. Sixty brings retirement for many people, and the freedom to spend time as they choose. Each decade birthday is a chance to look back at what has been achieved and forward to what comes next, and that is worth celebrating rather than dreading.

The grand milestones: 60, 70, 80, 90 and 100

Sixty, seventy and eighty are increasingly celebrated as achievements in themselves, as more people reach them in good health. In China, the sixtieth birthday is a major celebration, because it marks the completion of a full sixty-year cycle of the zodiac. In Korea, the sixtieth birthday, or hwangap, is one of the most important celebrations in life. In Japan, the sixtieth birthday is marked by wearing a red vest, a symbol of rebirth.

Ninety and one hundred are rare and remarkable. In Britain, reaching one hundred brings a congratulatory message from the monarch, a tradition that began in 1917. In Japan, centenarians receive a silver cup from the Prime Minister. These birthdays are celebrated by whole communities, because they represent a century of history lived through. If you are lucky enough to know a centenarian, their birthday is the most important party you will ever attend.

Frequently asked questions

What is a milestone birthday?
A birthday that marks a legal, cultural or personal turning point — usually 16, 18, 21, 30, 40, 50, 60 or 100.

Why is 21 a milestone in the US?
It is the legal drinking age, and traditionally the age of majority in Britain.

What is special about turning 60 in China?
It completes a full sixty-year zodiac cycle, and is a major celebration.
