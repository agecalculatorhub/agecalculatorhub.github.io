The Chinese Zodiac: Animals, Years and What They Mean

AgeCalculator guides

While the Western zodiac divides the year into twelve signs, the Chinese zodiac divides time into twelve years, each ruled by an animal. If you were born in 1990, you are a Horse; if you were born in 2000, you are a Dragon; and if you were born in 2024, you are a Dragon too, because the cycle repeats every twelve years. This guide explains the twelve animals, how the cycle works, and the traditions that surround it.

The twelve animals

The twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac, in order, are the Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog and Pig. The cycle begins with the Rat and ends with the Pig, then starts again. The most recent cycle began in 2020, the Year of the Rat, and will end in 2031, the Year of the Pig. Your animal is determined by your birth year in the Chinese lunar calendar, which usually begins in late January or early February — so someone born in January 1990 is a Snake, not a Horse, because the Year of the Horse had not yet begun.

Each animal has traditional traits. Rats are said to be clever and resourceful, Oxen hardworking and honest, Tigers brave and confident, Rabbits gentle and kind, Dragons powerful and lucky, Snakes wise and intuitive, Horses energetic and independent, Goats creative and gentle, Monkeys witty and curious, Roosters observant and honest, Dogs loyal and faithful, and Pigs generous and good-natured. These descriptions are part of a two-thousand-year-old tradition, and they are used in much the same way as Western star signs — as a cultural language for personality, not as a scientific claim.

The legend of the Great Race

The traditional story of the zodiac is the Great Race. The Jade Emperor invited all the animals to a race across a river, and the first twelve to finish would have a year named after them. The Rat won by riding on the Ox's back and jumping ahead at the last moment. The Tiger came third, the Rabbit fourth after hopping across on stones, and the Dragon fifth, delayed because it had stopped to help villagers. The Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog and Pig followed, and the Pig came last because it had stopped for a nap and a meal.

The story explains the order of the animals and something of their character: the Rat's cunning, the Ox's strength, the Dragon's kindness, the Pig's love of comfort. It is told to children across China and the Chinese diaspora, and it is one of the best-known legends in the world. The race is also why the Rat, the smallest animal, leads the cycle — a reminder that cleverness can beat strength.

Elements and the sixty-year cycle

Each Chinese zodiac year is also associated with one of five elements — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water — which rotate in a ten-year cycle. Combined with the twelve animals, this creates a sixty-year cycle in which each animal-element combination occurs only once. The year 2024 was a Wood Dragon, 2025 a Wood Snake, and 2026 a Fire Horse. Your full sign is therefore not just your animal but your animal and your element together, which is why two people born twelve years apart share an animal but have different elements.

The sixty-year cycle is the length of a full human lifetime in traditional Chinese thought, and completing a full cycle is a major milestone. It is also the reason the Chinese calendar can describe any year with just two characters — one for the animal and one for the element. The system is older than the Western zodiac and has been in continuous use for more than two thousand years.

Lunar New Year traditions

The Chinese zodiac year begins at the Lunar New Year, also called the Spring Festival, which falls between 21 January and 20 February. The festival lasts fifteen days and is the most important holiday in Chinese culture. Families gather for a reunion dinner, children receive red envelopes of money, and the streets fill with lanterns, fireworks and dragon dances. Each year, decorations, greetings and gifts feature the new animal, and people born in that animal's year celebrate their zodiac year — their benmingnian.

Your benmingnian, the year of your own animal, is traditionally considered a year of challenges, and people wear red for protection and good luck. The year is also a time for reflection: the cycle has come full circle, and you are the same animal you were twelve, twenty-four or thirty-six years ago. Whether or not you believe in the tradition, the Lunar New Year is a wonderful celebration of family, food and renewal, and the zodiac gives it a structure that has lasted for millennia.

Frequently asked questions

What animal am I in the Chinese zodiac?
It depends on your birth year — for example, 1990 is the Horse and 2000 is the Dragon.

When does the Chinese New Year start?
Between 21 January and 20 February each year, on the new moon.

What is my zodiac year?
The year of your own animal, which comes around every twelve years.
