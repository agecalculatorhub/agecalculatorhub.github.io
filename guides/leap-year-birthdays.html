Born on 29 February: What Leap Year Birthdays Are Really Like

AgeCalculator guides

About one person in 1,461 is born on 29 February, a date that appears on the calendar only once every four years. Leap day babies — sometimes called leaplings — grow up answering the same questions all their lives: when do you celebrate, how old are you really, and what happens on your birthday in ordinary years? This guide answers them all.

How leap years work

A leap year happens almost every four years, when an extra day is added to February to keep the calendar aligned with the Earth's orbit. The Earth takes 365.25 days to orbit the Sun, so without the extra day the calendar would drift by a day every four years, and by a month within a few centuries. The rule is: a year is a leap year if it is divisible by four, except for century years, which are leap years only if divisible by 400. So 2024 was a leap year, 2000 was a leap year, but 1900 and 2100 are not.

The 29 February birthday is therefore rare: the chance of being born on that date is about one in 1,461, because there are 1,461 days in a four-year cycle. There are about five million leaplings in the world, and they share a bond that most people find charming. Some leap day babies joke that they age four times more slowly than everyone else — a 40-year-old leapling has celebrated only ten real birthdays.

When do leap babies celebrate?

In non-leap years, leap day babies have to choose a day to celebrate, and the choice splits roughly evenly between 28 February and 1 March. There is no universal rule: some families celebrate on the 28th, the last day of February, and others on 1 March, the day after the birthday would have fallen. Some celebrate on both days, and some celebrate on the nearest weekend. The choice is personal, and most leaplings have a firm opinion about it.

The legal position varies by country. In the United Kingdom and the United States, the law generally treats 1 March as the birthday in non-leap years, which means a person born on 29 February 2008 turned 18 on 1 March 2026, not 28 February. In some other countries, the birthday is legally 28 February. For most practical purposes — driving licences, voting, school enrolment — the authorities use 1 March, but it is worth checking the rule in your own country if it matters.

Famous leaplings and leap day traditions

Famous people born on 29 February include the composer Gioachino Rossini, the actor Dennis Farina, the jazz musician Dinah Shore, and the motivational speaker Tony Robbins. Rossini is said to have joked that he was only 18 years old when he was 72. The town of Anthony in Texas calls itself the Leap Year Capital of the World and holds a festival every four years, where leaplings gather from around the world to celebrate together.

Leap day itself has its own traditions. In Ireland and Britain, tradition says that women may propose marriage on 29 February, a custom that dates back centuries. In Greece, it is considered unlucky to marry in a leap year. In some countries, leap day babies receive special treatment from businesses: some restaurants offer free meals, and some towns hold parties for them. The rarity of the date makes it a talking point, and most leaplings say they enjoy the attention.

Practical issues for leap day babies

The most common practical problem is computer systems. Some websites and forms do not accept 29 February as a valid date of birth, or they miscalculate the age of a leapling in non-leap years. Insurance, banking and government systems have mostly fixed this, but older systems still occasionally fail. If you are a leapling and a form rejects your birth date, try entering 28 February or 1 March and contacting the organisation to correct the record.

The other issue is documents: a driving licence or passport issued in a leap year may show an expiry date that does not exist in a non-leap year, though most authorities handle this automatically. And when it comes to celebrating, the best advice is to celebrate on whichever day you prefer — or to celebrate twice. After all, a birthday that comes once every four years deserves more than one day of attention.

Frequently asked questions

How rare is a 29 February birthday?
About one in 1,461 people — there are roughly five million leaplings worldwide.

When do leap day babies turn 18?
Usually on 1 March in the non-leap year, though the rule varies by country.

Is 2100 a leap year?
No — century years are leap years only if divisible by 400, so 2100 is not one.
