Birthstones for Every Month: History, Meaning and How to Choose

AgeCalculator guides

Every month has a birthstone, a gem associated with the people born in that month. January's is garnet, February's is amethyst, and December's is turquoise. The tradition is more than a thousand years old, and it has survived because it gives a personal, beautiful meaning to a birthday. This guide explains the stone for each month, where the tradition came from, and how to choose birthstone jewellery.

The traditional birthstones

The modern list of birthstones was standardised in 1912 by the American jewellery industry, and it has been updated a few times since. January is garnet, February is amethyst, March is aquamarine, April is diamond, May is emerald, June is pearl and alexandrite, July is ruby, August is peridot, September is sapphire, October is opal and tourmaline, November is topaz and citrine, and December is turquoise, tanzanite and zircon. Each stone has a colour, a history and a traditional meaning.

The stones were chosen for their beauty, their availability and their associations. Garnet is a deep red stone that was once believed to protect travellers. Amethyst is a purple quartz that was believed to prevent drunkenness. Aquamarine is a pale blue stone named for the sea. Diamond is the hardest natural material and the symbol of enduring love. Emerald is a vivid green stone that was Cleopatra's favourite. Ruby is a red stone that was once thought to be more valuable than diamond.

Where the tradition came from

The idea of associating gems with months is very old. It is often traced to the breastplate of the High Priest in the Bible, which held twelve stones representing the twelve tribes of Israel, and to the writings of the first-century historian Josephus, who linked the stones to the twelve signs of the zodiac. By the Middle Ages, people believed that wearing the stone of your birth month would bring good luck and protect you from harm, and that each stone had a special power in its own month.

The tradition spread through Europe and then to America, where the jewellery industry formalised the list in 1912. The list has been updated since — alexandrite was added for June, citrine for November, and tanzanite for December — but the core stones have remained the same for more than a century. Today, birthstones are one of the most popular ways to personalise jewellery, and a birthstone gift is a thoughtful choice for a birthday.

Choosing and caring for birthstone jewellery

When choosing birthstone jewellery, think about the wearer's taste as well as their birth month. Some birthstones are durable and suitable for everyday wear — diamond, sapphire, ruby and emerald are all hard stones — while others are softer and better suited to occasional wear: pearl, opal and turquoise are all relatively soft and need gentle handling. A ring worn every day should be a hard stone; a pendant or earrings can be a softer one.

Care for birthstone jewellery by cleaning it gently with warm soapy water and a soft brush, and storing it separately so that harder stones do not scratch softer ones. Avoid ultrasonic cleaners for opal, pearl, turquoise and emerald, which can be damaged by vibration or chemicals. And remember that a birthstone gift does not have to be expensive — a small garnet, amethyst or peridot can be beautiful and affordable, and the thought behind it is what matters most.

Birthstones and their meanings

Each birthstone carries a traditional meaning. Garnet means loyalty and friendship. Amethyst means calm and clarity. Aquamarine means courage and peace. Diamond means strength and love. Emerald means rebirth and good fortune. Pearl means purity and wisdom. Ruby means passion and protection. Peridot means strength and healing. Sapphire means wisdom and loyalty. Opal means hope and creativity. Topaz means warmth and affection. Turquoise means protection and good luck.

These meanings are traditional rather than scientific, but they make a birthstone gift more personal: you are not just giving a beautiful stone, you are giving a wish for the year ahead. Whether you believe in the meanings or not, a birthstone is a lovely way to mark a birthday, and a piece of jewellery that carries a personal meaning is more likely to be treasured than one that does not.
